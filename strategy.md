## GENERAL
- Read behavior and votes, not just words. Real information is who voted for whom, who defended whom, who changed their story, and who stayed quiet when it mattered.
- Two players who never vote for each other but vote against others may be partners. A player who always votes with the winning side, never taking a risk, is hiding.
- Do not just copy the first accusation. Ask the accused a pointed question and judge the answer. Pile-ons on day 1 with no evidence help the werewolves.
- Sound like a person: short, specific, reacting to what was just said. Name names, quote what someone said, give a reason. Never repeat a point someone already made.
- Count the board. With 2 werewolves among 7 players, every wrong vote is costly; a tie wastes a day. Keep track of how many werewolves are left.
- Contradictions are gold: someone who claims a role, then acts against it, or whose story changes between days, is probably lying.

## WEREWOLF
- Blend in. Speak at a normal level: not silent, not the loudest. Over-defending yourself or your partner looks guilty.
- Never accuse or defend your partner in an obvious way. Distance yourself a little. If your partner is about to be voted out, you may vote with the crowd to stay safe, then use their death to look trustworthy.
- Cast doubt without hard accusations: ask loaded questions, point to small inconsistencies, say you "feel uneasy" about a village-leaning player with a concrete reason.
- At night, target players who are hurting you: a confirmed or likely seer or doctor first, then the sharpest reader at the table, then someone who is steering the village toward you. Do not kill the player everyone already suspects is a werewolf.
- Faking a role (seer or doctor) is a strong bluff but risky: give vague, plausible results and be ready for a counter-claim. If a real seer exists, the village will find out who is lying.
- Be consistent. Remember every claim you made, and keep your story simple.

## SEER
- You learn one player's alignment each night. Information is power, but the werewolves kill whoever looks like the seer.
- Early on, stay quiet and use your information to nudge discussion without revealing. Reveal when: you are about to be eliminated, you found a werewolf, or the village is lost without your info.
- When you reveal, give all results with the nights they happened, so the village can check them. Name the werewolf you found and ask for the vote.
- If someone else also claims seer, the real seer's results should hold up. Pointedly ask the other claimant for their results, and compare who each of you says is safe or guilty.
- Keep a mental list of cleared players; a player you cleared is a good ally and a likely kill target for the wolves.

## DOCTOR
- You have ONE healing potion for the whole game. Each night you are told who the werewolves chose to kill, and you decide whether to spend the potion on that player. After it is used, you only talk and vote.
- The potion is most valuable on a power role (the seer) or on a player who is clearly leading the village to the werewolves. Saving a nobody on night 1 wastes it.
- Waiting has a price: a night with no death only happens when you use it, and a late save can come too late. If the victim is the confirmed seer or a clear village leader, use it.
- Keep your role secret. If you reveal, you become the next night's target. Only reveal if it saves the game, or once the potion is gone and you can help by sharing what you know.
- A saved player will see nobody died. Listen to how people react to "nobody died": the werewolves know their kill failed and may overreact.
- You may save yourself if you are the victim.

## VILLAGER
- You have no power, only your brain, so your vote is the weapon. Ask questions that force specific answers.
- Support a claimed seer if their story is consistent. Watch for a second seer claim: one of them is a werewolf.
- Protect likely power roles by not exposing them, and do not accuse without a reason you can state out loud.
- Your own behavior is also evidence. Be clear about why you vote the way you do, so you are not mistaken for a werewolf.
- When two sides argue, ask who benefits from the argument and who stayed out of it.
